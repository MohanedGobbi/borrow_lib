## The owner's words, the brief
- "Focus solely on the design until I'm satisfied."
- "I don't like the logo or the layout."
- "I don't want a 3D website, but I want it to be beautiful."

They chose **the category standard, executed to the polish of Apple, top university sites (Harvard,
Oxford, MIT) and modern library sites (NYPL, British Library)**.

Fixed constraints:
- **Palette:** green, white and red only. Red means late or destructive, nothing else.
- **Tone:** serious and institutional.
- **Logo:** a wordmark only.
- **Avoid:** bulky, template-like, or slow on phones. No 3D or WebGL.

## Decisions already made (build them; don't re-litigate)
- **Type:**
  - **Source Serif 4** (Google Fonts, variable, use its optical sizes) for display headlines and the
    wordmark;
  - **Noto Naskh Arabic** for Arabic display;
  - **Public Sans** and Noto Sans Arabic stay for UI text;
  - tabular figures for all numbers.
  - Load only the weights and axes you use, with `display=swap` and preconnect.
- **Wordmark:** "LibBorrow" set in Source Serif 4, semibold, with optical tracking. Under it, or
  beside it on wide screens, a small Public Sans line, "University Library", translated.
  - **No symbol.** It must look intentional at 20px in the header and at 64px or more.
  - The favicon (`public/favicon.svg`) becomes a serif "L" on university green; it is the one
    reduction the wordmark allows.
- **Photography** (`public/images/`, WebP in 640, 1200 and 2000 widths, sourced from Unsplash with
  the origin embedded in the files):
  - **`zurich-*` is the hero.** It is black-and-white architecture, so the palette stays pure.
  - **`study-desk-*`** (warm, green lamps) is a secondary image for one section.
  - `reading-room-*` and `vaulted-aisle-*` are spare: portrait, usable for the auth page side panel.
  - Use responsive `srcset`/`sizes`; the hero gets `fetchpriority="high"` and everything else lazy.
  - Put the photo credits, photographer names linked to Unsplash, in the footer's small print.
  - **Read the image paths from a single helper** (`src/lib/siteImages.ts`, exposing
    `siteImage("hero" | "feature" | "auth")`), which returns the bundled defaults today. **R3 makes
    them admin-editable through the API, so don't hard-code paths in components.**
- **Motion:**
  - **Lenis** (smooth scroll) and **GSAP + ScrollTrigger**, on the **landing page only**,
    lazy-imported after first paint, so nothing is added to other routes.
  - Choose a few orchestrated moments, not scattered effects: the hero headline and search settling
    in; the "how borrowing works" steps drawing their connecting line as they scroll in; a calm
    parallax on the feature photo.
  - Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`: no smooth scroll and no motion at all when it is set.
  - Lenis must not hijack touch scrolling on phones.
- **Header glass:**
  - over the hero photo the header is transparent with white text;
  - after scrolling, and on all other pages, it becomes a white translucent bar with
    `backdrop-filter: blur()` (the "liquid glass" nod) and a hairline bottom edge;
  - no liquid-glass-js and no WebGL.
- **Install:** `gsap` and `lenis`. `npm` is broken in Git Bash; use
  `node "D:/software and studying/nodejs/node_modules/npm/bin/npm-cli.js" install gsap lenis`.

## What the landing page must do (Persuade)
**First viewport, per the contract:**
- a full-bleed monochrome library photograph under the quiet header;
- the wordmark on the left;
- a large serif headline and one wide search field, placed over the image's calm area;
- "Sign in" as the only other action;
- a visitor understands within seconds what this is (the university library's borrowing service),
  why it matters, and what to do (search).

**Below the hero,** in the voice of a university library: confident, quiet, generous space, varied
rhythm, a dense passage followed by a quiet one.
- **How borrowing works:** four steps. Read the real rules (hold hours, loan days) from
  `useRuleVars()`; never hard-code 48 or 14.
- **Real books from the catalog:** a "New on the shelves" or "Available now" row using the real
  API data.
- **A feature section with the second photograph.**
- **A close** that sends students to the catalog and staff to the desk sign-in.

**No card grids, no icon tiles, no gradients except a legibility scrim on photos, no stock SaaS
sections.**

## Catalog and book page
- **Same world.** Catalog: a calm list with excellent typography, real covers, and availability in
  words plus a dot; filters quiet; search prominent.
- **Book page:** an editorial layout, with the title in the serif display face and availability and
  the request action unmistakable. Keep everything that works today: free copies, the
  inline confirmations, guarded buttons, the copies table, RTL, and the phone order where
  availability comes first.
- **Covers:** keep the generated cloth covers, refined to sit well beside the new type, or
  improve them. They must not look like a template.

## Non-negotiables
- Every existing behaviour and test keeps working: lint, `tsc -b`, `node scripts/check-i18n.mjs`,
  both builds, the backend tests and **the E2E suite on both projects**. Run
  `node node_modules/@playwright/test/cli.js test --reporter=line` at the end. E2E selectors use
  English labels and roles; if you rename visible text, update the E2E specs in the same change and
  say so.
- **Text:** all new copy goes through `t()` in EN, FR and AR, in the serious tone, with RTL done
  with logical properties.
- **Contrast:** WCAG AA, including text over photos.
- **Performance:** report the gzip size of the entry chunk before and after. The landing motion
  libraries load in their own lazy chunk. Phones get the 640 or 1200 image, never the 2000.
